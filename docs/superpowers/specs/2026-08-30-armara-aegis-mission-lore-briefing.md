# Armara Aegis Mission Lore Briefing

**Status:** Approved amendment

**Date:** 2026-08-30

**Amends:** The campaign expansion specification's player-facing briefing contract

## Player-facing contract

Every mission briefing presents one short historical-fiction synopsis. It does not divide the story into Situation, Road, Your Task, or What is new here. The act and historical era remain as a compact kicker; mission statistics, Laurel targets, loadout, and start controls remain unchanged.

The synopsis is story, not instruction copy. Route descriptions, objectives, mechanic notices, and other structured briefing fields remain in content for validation and future contextual help, but the briefing does not render them. Each synopsis is one plain paragraph, readable at roughly 70 characters per line, and must not expose grid coordinates, pad quality, runtime IDs, ticks, or hashes.

Historical people, places, and events ground the campaign. Every battle described below is fictional unless it is an established historical event. Mythic figures and uncertain attributions are never presented as documented fact. A future post-victory Codex may distinguish the historical anchor from Armara's fiction and carry source credits; citations do not clutter the pre-battle briefing.

## Canonical mission synopses

### Act I — Attican Boot Sequence

1. **Gate of Dawn.** Before word of Marathon reaches Athens, a second hostile column appears on the dawn road. Miltiades' exhausted runners reach the gate first, and the city's hastily raised defenders must hold until the army returns.
2. **Agora Circuit.** As Athenians gather to put Kleisthenes' new civic order into practice, saboteurs slip through the colonnades to silence the vote. Kleisthenes refuses to empty the square, turning the Agora itself into a circuit of barricades around the newborn democracy.
3. **Olive Cipher.** A cipher hidden in amphora seals reveals an assault timed to the fleet's departure. Themistocles sends a small guard through Athena's olive groves to keep the signal fires alive until the triremes clear the straits.
4. **Piraeus Switchyard.** With Piraeus still half wall and half shipyard, enemy detachments strike both unfinished harbours at once. Themistocles turns cranes, ramps, and causeways into a switching defense long enough to launch the fleet.
5. **Bronze Warden.** Pheidias receives bronze from captured weapons for a guardian commission, but an ancient core awakens inside the mould. The defenders descend through the foundry spiral as Talos tears free piece by piece.

### Act II — Shards of the Aegean

6. **Delos Airspace.** As the first allied tribute reaches sacred Delos, winged machines descend upon the harbour and the Terrace of Lions. Aristides must protect the League's treasury without turning Apollo's sanctuary into a fortress.
7. **Naxos Labyrinth.** A Naxian captain opens hidden lanes through the mosaic quarter while Athenian ships close the harbour. With both sides claiming to protect the League, the defenders must decide which crossings remain open.
8. **Poseidon's Clock.** At Sounion, watchers discover that the assault changes course with each tide bell. Temple guards must read sea, shadow, and beacon to choose which road to reinforce before every new landing.
9. **Rhodes Ring.** After Demetrius abandons his siege, the immense Helepolis begins turning by itself and casting solar shields across the ring road. Chares orders the captured machine dismantled before its bronze can become Helios' monument.
10. **Cyclops Kernel.** Beneath Tiryns, builders breach a chamber older than the citadel and wake the "Cyclops" the colossal stones were meant to contain. Every spoke road carries a different fragment toward one rebuilding core.

### Act III — Oracle War

11. **Thermopylae Firewall.** While Leonidas holds the main pass, a forgotten signal road begins transmitting the defenders' positions. Six emplacements must delay a second advance long enough to destroy the beacon before the encirclement closes.
12. **Delphi Duplex.** Two contradictory prophecies leave Delphi by separate sacred roads, and each draws an army determined to seize the "true" answer. The sanctuary guard must keep both tablets from the Oracle Core until their alteration is exposed.
13. **Forge Perimeter.** Miltiades expects Hephaestia to yield, but the volcanic workshops lock themselves and begin forging an army. He must defend the workers on the outer road while scouts discover who—or what—issued the order.
14. **Acropolis Mesh.** During Athens' rebuilding, Pheidias finds enemy shield patterns hidden in the new marble measurements. Moonlit attackers climb mirrored approaches while masons turn unfinished colonnades into a defense.
15. **Oracle's Black Box.** Philomelus opens a sealed chamber beneath Delphi, and every oracle begins changing after it is spoken. As Philip approaches, the defenders descend the double spiral to sever the device before its false answers ignite another war.

### Act IV — Titan Singularity

16. **Styx Packet Loss.** Pausanias follows the Arcadian waterfall into a buried channel where lost shades vanish between crossings. Charon's ferrymen hold the portals while the defenders restore the broken route.
17. **Trident Convergence.** At Artemisium, three coastal lanes carry survivors, armour, and signals while the storm scatters both fleets. Themistocles must keep the prongs from converging on the evacuation road before news of Leonidas arrives.
18. **Titan Assembly.** An unnamed Hellenistic engineer discovers that three workshop nodes are assembling a war engine from calendar gears. Couriers must be intercepted before the final astronomical cycle closes and Titan Prime wakes.
19. **Olympus Uplink.** On the eve of Alexander's Asian campaign, the lamps at Dion answer lightning no priest summoned. The defenders climb the switchbacks to sever a false uplink before it turns the king's departure rite into catastrophe.
20. **Eternal Singularity.** Armara's archive folds every battlefield into Hesiod's last unwritten verse. Chronarch tries to repeat the Titanomachy forever, while heroes preserved from all four acts hold the omega road and rebuild Zeus' final thunder.

## Acceptance criteria

- The briefing contains exactly one mission synopsis paragraph.
- "What is new here," "The situation," "The road," and "Your task" do not appear.
- The three compiled missions use their canonical synopsis above.
- All 20 canonical synopses are available to future mission authors in this amendment.
- Mission essentials and keyboard order remain intact.
- The complete Aegis test suite stays green.
